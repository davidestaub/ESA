\begin{abstract}
Transmission spectroscopy probes the composition of exoplanet atmospheres
through the wavelength dependence of the transit depth. This spectrum is inferred through a chain of detector calibration, background treatment, extraction and
light-curve fitting. Independent pipelines can disagree on the same
observation, and agreement cannot establish accuracy, because a real planet's
true spectrum is unknown. The chain estimates the background before the
transit fit and compresses each image to one dimension, two choices whose
effect on recovery accuracy this project tests. It asks how accurately JWST
NIRISS/SOSS transmission spectra can be recovered from detector-level data,
and whether fitting the transit and the background jointly in the pixels reduces the
biases of extraction-first analyses. For this joint fit, I have developed
Nonlinear Order-coupled Variable-projection Analysis (NOVA). It passes a
trial transmission spectrum, shared by both spectral orders, through an
empirical model of the instrument to predict every pixel's count rate, and
adjusts the spectrum until the prediction matches the data. NOVA's first, preliminary spectrum
of the hot Jupiter WASP-17~b has features similar to the published one, but is
deeper at all wavelengths, most of all in the red. Its full
uncertainty is not yet validated. Because real data cannot settle such differences, I built injection benchmarks on real SOSS data with a known transit. Injection requires knowing
which light is stellar, because only starlight dims. For one tested
atmosphere, the ranking of two pipelines reverses with the assumed stellar
fraction, which the WASP-17~b data leave undetermined. I am therefore building
a benchmark from programme 4476, which observed a star at its usual position
and again moved off the detector strip, so the difference measures its light.
Its cleaned image, with a known light curve and photon noise, will be added to
the moved exposure's raw data, making recovery tests conditional on this
construction. The rest of the PhD will use this benchmark to score NOVA
against published pipelines, apply NOVA to five further SOSS visits and repeated visits of the
same planets, and extend it to NIRSpec and secondary eclipses.
\end{abstract}
