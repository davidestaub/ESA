\section{Discussion and Research Plan}
\label{sec:research-plan}

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The main lesson of the first year concerns the benchmark rather than any one
pipeline. An injection test on real SOSS data has to decide which light belongs to the star. For one atmosphere injected into the WASP-17~b data, this decision reversed the ranking of the two
pipelines I tested (Section~\ref{sec:which-light}). Every injection test into real data makes such a decision, stated or not, and its verdict only holds
under it. The benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:measured-benchmark} constrains this decision with a separate measurement of the star. The price is a different
star, field and readout and, at first, a grey transit, so it cannot say how
the pipelines behave on WASP-17 itself. It is designed instead to test which
kinds of error each method makes when the injected light curve is known. The
comparison of the six science visits will be read against these errors.

NOVA's first spectrum of the real WASP-17~b visit lies above the published
spectrum at every wavelength and most in the red
(Section~\ref{sec:nova-real}). Its uncertainty is not yet validated, since the
first ensemble failed its held-out test and the second was not completed. One further lead remains open. In the red, the faint light far from the traces of WASP-17 appears to dim during the transit by more than predictions of the stellar halo suggest. This excess is sensitive to the baseline and the processing, and whether it comes from the star, the background or the detector is unresolved. The new benchmark will measure recovery errors under its declared conditions, but the accuracy of either real WASP-17~b spectrum remains unknown.

\subsection{Research plan}
\label{sec:plan}

The plan therefore begins with the measured-starlight benchmark and a refit of WASP-17~b whose uncertainty will be tested on held-out realisations.
These form the first of five papers (Figure~\ref{fig:gantt}), all built on
NOVA and this benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:measured-benchmark}). The second
applies the same procedure to the five other science visits, and the third
turns to repeated visits of the same planets. The last two extend NOVA to
NIRSpec and to secondary eclipses.

\paragraph{Paper 1: NOVA, the benchmark and WASP-17~b.} The aim is to test
whether NOVA recovers transmission spectra more accurately than the
extraction-based pipelines, and whether it changes the conclusions about
WASP-17~b. Firstly, I will build and test the benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:measured-benchmark} and score NOVA and exoTEDRF on it,
together with Ahsoka and \texttt{transitspectroscopy} where their native
workflows run end to end. The benchmark tests how each method separates
starlight from background and extracts the spectrum. Its first test uses one
common light curve on an A-type star, with the limb-darkening controls and
limits of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-limits}, and does not by itself validate
recovery for the cooler science targets. Secondly, I will refit NOVA's
spectrum of the real WASP-17~b visit with the current geometry criterion and
compare it with the three published reductions. Its uncertainty will come from
an ensemble that corresponds to the updated fit and passes its held-out test.
I aim to finish this paper before the HWO2026 conference in Paris at the
end of November 2026, where I will present this work.

\paragraph{Paper 2: Five further SOSS visits.} The aim is to find out whether
NOVA changes the atmospheric conclusions for other SOSS targets, and if so,
why. The five remaining science visits, of HAT-P-26~b, WASP-39~b, HAT-P-18~b,
WASP-96~b and WASP-80~b, will be analysed as real data with NOVA and the extraction-based pipelines of Paper~1. WASP-39~b has strong water features and well-studied spectra
\citep{FeinsteinEtAl2023,CarterEtAl2024}, whilst the published analyses of
WASP-96~b disagree on whether its spectrum shows a scattering slope
\citep{TaylorEtAl2023,WangEtAl2026}. Neither agreement nor disagreement on real data will identify the more accurate pipeline, so the differences between the pipelines will be read against the errors measured in Paper~1. All five will use a common
analysis procedure, with any visit-specific settings declared before the
recovered spectra are compared. Applying the same procedure to these visits also requires a declared treatment of field stars, which NOVA does not
yet model. Where they contaminate the traces, I will first exclude the
affected columns, and model their spectra only where the loss of coverage
matters for the science. This procedure can later be extended to the rest of
the public SOSS archive, as was done for Hubble data \citep{TsiarasEtAl2018}.

\paragraph{Paper 3: Repeated visits and stellar variability.} The aim is to
test whether repeated visits of the same planet give the same spectrum once
instrumental and stellar effects are accounted for. I will start with the two
SOSS transits of GJ~9827~d \citep{PiauletGhorayebEtAl2024}, then turn to the
four of LHS~1140~b \citep{CadieuxEtAl2024,GressierEtAl2026} and the five of
TRAPPIST-1~f, each of which contains a flare \citep{LimEtAl2026}. NOVA will
gain a flare model, with timing shared across wavelengths and brightness
fitted at each wavelength, and a model of unocculted spots and faculae. Two
planned MRes projects, starting in January 2027, will study stellar
variability and flares in the out-of-transit SOSS data, which would feed
directly into this paper.

\paragraph{Paper 4: Joint NIRISS and NIRSpec analysis.} The aim is to test
whether observations of the same planet with both instruments can be explained
by one transmission spectrum. Spectra from different instruments agree better
when analysed with the same choices \citep{CarterEtAl2024}, but such agreement
does not show that the choices are right. I will therefore extend NOVA and the
benchmark to NIRSpec, validate each mode on injected data, and only then
compare the real spectra, starting with WASP-39~b and LHS~1140~b
\citep{DamianoEtAl2024}.

\paragraph{Paper 5: Secondary eclipses.} The final extension changes the event
being measured from a transit to a secondary eclipse, to find out whether a
detector-level analysis makes weak eclipse measurements more reliable.
WASP-17~b is the starting point, since the retrieved properties of its SOSS
eclipse spectrum were sensitive to whether negative eclipse depths were
allowed at optical wavelengths \citep{GressierEtAl2024}. NOVA will use an
eclipse model with signed amplitudes, and the benchmark will add a known
planetary signal that disappears during the eclipse.

\paragraph{Dependencies and risks.} Paper 1 provides the benchmark that every
later paper reuses. Paper 2 settles the treatment of field stars, and Paper 3
the stellar model that the later papers need for active hosts. In every paper,
the recovered spectra are fixed before any retrieval is run, so that they
cannot be tuned towards an atmospheric answer.

The first risk is that an injected case fails one of its tests. Such a case would
be reported and excluded, with the scope of the remaining benchmark stated,
whilst a pipeline that fails on a valid case keeps that failure in its score.
Both the three- and four-group versions of the benchmark will be reported.

A second risk is that the rerun uncertainty ensemble fails its held-out test
again. The spectrum of WASP-17~b would then be published without a validated
uncertainty, or not at all, and I would state which. Contamination by field
stars may also remove more wavelength coverage than a science question can
tolerate, in which case the affected spectra are released with reduced
coverage and the limitation stated.

The NIRSpec extension is the largest technical development of the later
papers. If it does not validate in time, Paper 4 is reduced to comparing
independently validated reductions of the two instruments, with their
limitations stated. Papers 4 and 5 are the adjustable part of the plan.
Finally, NOVA may not outperform the extraction-based pipelines. A fair
benchmark would still be useful, because it would document the methods' errors
under the tested conditions.
