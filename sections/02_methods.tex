\section{Methods and Benchmark Design}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Experimental design and data scope}

The initial benchmark is based on the JWST/NIRISS SOSS observation of
WASP-17 b. Method development uses detector-level products derived from this
observation together with controlled synthetic transit signals. Later benchmark
stages will expand the atmospheric inputs and observing conditions while
preserving a common comparison protocol.

\subsection{Ground-truth detector-level injector}

The injector constructs a time-dependent stellar contribution on the detector,
applies a known transmission spectrum through the transit model, and combines
the result with additive background and the required detector information. The
same synthetic detector cube is supplied to every recovery method. Injector and
recovery components are kept separate so that the known spectrum is not used by
the fit.

\subsection{The \nova{} detector-level forward model}

NOVA models the calibrated detector time series directly. A shared
wavelength-dependent transmission spectrum is projected through the instrument
response into both spectral orders and combined with a time-dependent stellar
model. The astrophysical and detector components are therefore fitted before
the detector measurements are collapsed into spectral light curves.

\subsection{Background and nuisance model}

The background remains additive in the detector model and is fitted jointly
with the stellar transit signal. Linear nuisance components describe the
stellar continuum and structured background, while the nonlinear parameters
describe the transmission spectrum, limb darkening, and other astrophysical
quantities required by the transit model.

\subsection{Profiled optimisation and robust fitting}

At fixed nonlinear parameters, NOVA eliminates the linear nuisance parameters
through variable projection. The remaining nonlinear problem is solved with
bounded optimisation inside a robust iteratively reweighted fit. Independent
deterministic starts and fixed prediction records are used to distinguish
optimisation behaviour from later evaluation against the injected spectrum.

\subsection{Extraction-based comparison analyses}

Extraction-based analyses receive the same detector cube used by NOVA. Their
calibration, background, extraction, and light-curve modelling stages are
documented separately so that differences in the recovered spectra can be
related to specific analysis choices.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

Recovery performance is evaluated using absolute and shape-sensitive spectral
errors, order-specific diagnostics, uncertainty calibration, and detector-domain
fit statistics. Comparisons are made only after each recovery has produced a
fixed prediction.
