\section{Benchmark Construction and Reproducibility}
\label{app:benchmarking}

\subsection{Frozen V45 detector cube}

The frozen control is the V45 integration-level benchmark constructed from
out-of-transit observations of WASP-17. It contains 229 integrations. Synthetic
transit modulation is applied to integrations 51 through 177, inclusive, while
the remaining 102 integrations form the event complement used by the injector
and by designated recovery-side calibrations. The delivered products retain the
Stage-2 science values, uncertainties, and data-quality flags. No frozen
background subtraction is undone or reapplied during delivery.

The injector preserves the realised detector noise, additive background, field
sources, higher-order structure, and temporal variability in the real
out-of-transit data. It separately estimates a denoised target-source expectation
for each spectral order. If $S^{\mathrm{emp}}_{o,tp}$ is that expectation and
$f_{o,tp}$ is the fractional in-transit source factor propagated to detector
pixel $p$, the delivered cube is
\begin{equation}
 Y^{\mathrm{inj}}_{tp}=Y^{\mathrm{OOT}}_{tp}
 +\sum_o S^{\mathrm{emp}}_{o,tp}\left(f_{o,tp}-1\right).
 \label{eq:v45-injector}
\end{equation}
Only the target-source expectation is multiplied by the transit factor.
Additive background, unrelated sources, and the realised noise are not made to
transit. Order contributions are constructed separately and summed in detector
space, including in overlap pixels.

The V45 writer uses the current PASTASOSS trace and wavelength solution
\citep{BainesEtAl2023Trace,BainesEtAl2023Wavelength} together with the
WebbKernel implementation of the ATOCA response mapping
\citep{DarveauBernierEtAl2022,JWSTPipeline2025}. Its support logic is fail
closed: empirical target light that lacks a valid model response causes the
injection to fail rather than being silently clipped or assigned a unity
transit factor. The source expectation is denoised before modulation so that
the injector does not attenuate a particular noise realisation.

\subsection{Generator, delivery, recovery, and scoring separation}

The benchmark is divided into four stages. The generator owns the atmospheric
truth, generator-only source model, and detector response. Delivery writes one
opaque detector cube and a manifest of the products available to every recovery
method. Recovery owns its calibration products, configuration, optimiser state,
diagnostics, and final detector prediction. Scoring reads the truth only after
that prediction has been closed. The same delivered cube is used unchanged by
NOVA-S and by each extraction-first comparison. The injector cannot inspect any
recovery aperture, q-star product, recovered spectrum, or truth residual.

Truth is unavailable to response construction, masks, nuisance-basis selection,
starting-point selection, model selection, convergence tests, and prediction
closure. A valid closure records the recovered spectrum on every declared grid,
the detector-prediction identity, convergence status, configuration and source
hashes, and an immutable timestamped manifest. A failed or non-strict fit may be
scored for diagnosis, but it cannot enter the primary comparison. Any method
change made after truth access defines a new benchmark branch and requires a new
hidden case before it can support a performance claim.

The prospective repair of the documented NOVA-S response defect follows the
same closure rules: one global rule must be applied to all columns, calibrated
on alternating OOT folds, and selected by held-out OOT detector likelihood. It
must preserve flux, order-2 performance, and the headline gates, and it must be
sealed before any transit error is read. The known defect may motivate the
class of repair that is tested, but its recovery score cannot select the
result.

\subsection{Common reporting support}

All methods are compared on the same physical $R=100$ grid and with the same
prospectively defined support mask. The order-1 PASTASOSS training anchors for
this visit end at detector coordinate $x=73.62893$, corresponding to
$2.75977449\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. Columns 38 through 73 therefore depend on polynomial
extrapolation and are not admitted to the primary paper comparison. The R7 rule
excludes any reporting bin with even partial dependence on those columns. It
retains 141 complete bins; the final retained bin ends at approximately
$2.740018\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. The mask is fixed by the instrument calibration domain,
applied symmetrically to all methods, and evaluated only after recovery.

Primary spectral accuracy is the unweighted absolute RMSE on these common bins.
Mean bias and morphology RMSE are reported separately so that an achromatic
offset is not confused with an incorrect spectral shape. Order-specific,
overlap, crossing-region, and supported-red diagnostics accompany the aggregate
score. Weighted RMSE is a sensitivity statistic rather than the headline
metric. Engineering cubes, including zero-injection, flat-depth, and noiseless
tests, are reported separately from sealed science scores.

\subsection{Rateints limitation and paired group-stage experiment}

V45 is an integration-level control. It does not contain the nondestructive
detector groups from which the count-rate images were fitted. Pipelines such as
Ahsoka and supreme-SPOON/exoTEDRF normally perform characteristic $1/f$
corrections before or during ramp fitting, so they cannot reproduce that step
authentically from V45 alone. A comparison on V45 can therefore test recovery
from an identical integration-level cube, but it cannot by itself determine the
effect of group-stage preprocessing on the ranking of methods.

A paired sensitivity experiment is consequently planned. One authenticated
group-level baseline will be used to construct matched group-stage and
integration-stage injections from the same physical source deficit. Each
pipeline will retain its own truth-blind preprocessing, while NOVA will also be
run after the same group-stage $1/f$ correction. The comparison will test
zero-injection identity, delivered detector deficit, white-light scatter,
red-end recovery, and common-bin RMSE. This paired experiment is not part of the
frozen NOVA-S result and has not yet passed the delivery and cross-pipeline
promotion gates.

The current scene-enabled injector candidate is likewise not promoted. Its
broad full-column exclusions remove a material fraction of the target signal,
so contaminant handling must be replaced by target-protecting, OOT-only scene
modelling before it can define a new public cube. The frozen V45 cube remains
the benchmark control unless a sealed fairness experiment demonstrates that a
reissue is required.

\subsection{Structural-neutrality and ensemble requirements}

The V45 injector and NOVA-S use related families of trace geometry, wavelength
mapping, and spectral mixing. Their code and fitted detector response are not
identical, but this shared modelling ancestry creates an inverse-crime risk.
The final benchmark programme will therefore include sealed injections with
deliberately mismatched spatial profiles, wavelength maps, line-spread
functions, throughput, background morphology, and temporal systematics.

WASP-17 is a development visit rather than sufficient validation by itself.
Additional visits must pass a fixed admission audit covering target and visit
identity, observing mode, out-of-transit coverage, integration chronology,
data quality, background structure, field sources, and order-3 morphology.
The same injected spectrum will first be recovered across the admitted visits,
followed by additional spectral morphologies and a method-neutral noise
ensemble. At least one visit must remain held out from model-branch selection.
Until these tests and the full pipeline matrix are complete, V45 supports a
controlled single-visit result rather than a universal ranking of reduction
methods.

\subsection{Provenance and minimum report}

Every release records content hashes for code, configuration, calibration and
response products, stellar-source and background products, detector arrays,
masks, closed predictions, and score tables. Large cluster-only arrays may be
represented in the portable archive by authenticated manifests. Superseded,
diagnostic, failed, and unpromoted branches retain an explicit status so that
they cannot be mistaken for the frozen method.
