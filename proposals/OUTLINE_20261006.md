# ESA outline (6 Oct 2026; due 12 Oct; 20 pages including references)

This outline follows Davide's decisions of 6 Oct, recorded in `AGENT_BOARD/DECISIONS.md`. There is no appendix. The old Results and Conclusions sections are removed. Writing follows `STYLE_RULES_20261006.md`.

**The story in one paragraph.** Pipelines produce different SOSS spectra for the same planet, and real data cannot say which is right. I built NOVA, which fits the transit and the background together in detector space. On real WASP-17 b data it agrees with the published spectrum in the blue but not in the red, and without a known truth that difference cannot be resolved. A benchmark with known injected truth should answer it. My first benchmarks taught me three lessons:
1. inject into the raw ramps;
2. the injector must know which light belongs to the star;
3. the injector must not share the assumptions of any method it tests.

The second lesson is decisive. In WASP-17 data the star's faint light cannot be separated from the background, and the pipeline ranking flipped with the assumption. The new benchmark measures the star's light instead, using a calibration programme in which the star was moved off the detector strip.

### 3. Building a benchmark with known truth

- **What a benchmark needs:**
  - a known injected truth;
  - real raw data, so that the noise, flags, background and field sources are real;
  - identical inputs for every method, each fitting its own geometry;
  - the truth revealed only after each prediction is fixed;
  - an injector that shares no assumptions with any method under test (the "inverse crime").
- **Lesson 1.** The first injector worked on integration-level products, and so bypassed the detector stages in which the pipelines differ. I moved injection to the raw ramps. Source: AGENTS.md, 8 Sep.
- **Lesson 2.** To inject a transit, the injector dims the star's light only. In WASP-17 data the faint light far from the traces cannot be attributed to the star or to the background. With a lower and an upper estimate of the starlight, the ranking flips:
  - NOVA 124 to 358 ppm;
  - exoTEDRF 319 to 150 ppm.

  Both shift by about 500 ppm in the red, because only the in-transit decrement changes. Source: `NOVA_ASTRA_EXECUTION_20260905_R1/reports/OVERNIGHT_CLAUDE_LOG_20260925.md`; numbers to be re-checked.
- **Lesson 3.** NOVA's background model shares the lower estimate's assumption that about 2–4% of the red box light is not starlight. The lower-estimate benchmark therefore flatters NOVA (an inverse crime).
- **Bridge.** Estimating the split more carefully cannot fix this, because one component (a transit-shaped term that is uniform along each column) is not identifiable from the data. The starlight has to be measured. Source: board thread of 29 Sep; to be checked.
- **Figure:** the flip, with recovered-minus-true spectra for both pipelines under both estimates.

### 4. A benchmark with measured starlight

- **Programme 4476.** A SOSS aperture-correction calibration of TYC 4213-1116-1. It has a full-frame exposure with the star at the normal position (10 integrations) and one with the star displaced by 1,026 rows (150 integrations).
- **Measuring the star.** The normal exposure minus the displaced exposure gives the star's own light, including its far wings. The sky cancels to about 1%.
- **Construction:**
  - the carrier is the displaced exposure's raw ramps over the SOSS strip, with real detector effects, sky, field stars, 1/f noise and cosmic rays;
  - the cleaned, measured star is added with a known transit and photon noise;
  - every case has a paired no-transit twin;
  - there are 3- and 4-group versions.
- **Cleaning the star.**
  - Field sources: 48 in the displaced strip, including a field spectrum crossing order 1 at columns 849–1062 at up to 0.62% of the star's flux; and a zeroth order with a 4.5 DN/s peak in the normal strip.
  - Gaia-predicted traces locate them.
  - The sky difference is about 1%.
  - Far wings are modelled beyond a signal-to-noise of about 5.
- **Figure (three panels):** the normal exposure; the displaced exposure with the Gaia-predicted field traces; their difference, the measured star.
- **Tests before scoring:**
  - injecting nothing returns the carrier;
  - the transit and its twin are identical outside the transit;
  - the photon noise is correct;
  - the grey (common-curve) case comes first.
- **Limits:**
  - a different star and field from WASP-17;
  - full-frame data relabelled as the standard subarray;
  - 3–4 groups against 8;
  - the truth is known conditional on the cleaned star template;
  - field sources directly under the trace move with the star and cannot be separated;
  - with only 10 normal integrations, the far wings in the red have a signal-to-noise of 2–3 per pixel at 40–200 rows.
- **The six science visits** are run as real data. Differences between pipelines are reported, but without truth they are not assigned to a method.
