\section{\nova{} Implementation Details}
\label{app:nova}

\subsection{Provenance and input identities}

NOVA-S is an immutable release: a manifest authenticates 419 files covering
code, configuration, calibration products, predictions, and diagnostics,
and the runtime manifest pins every software dependency, including the
JWST calibration pipeline \citep{JWSTPipeline2025} and the transit engine
\citep{Jaxoplanet2025}. Internal archive identifiers appear only here for
byte-level traceability: the release is the internal V50/QE/FULL lineage,
the benchmark cube is the V45 archive, the background model is the E13b
family, and the reporting mask is the R7 product. Diagnostics and
candidate models live outside the frozen directory. The trace and
wavelength solution is the current public calibration
\citep{BainesEtAl2023Trace,BainesEtAl2023Wavelength} with the associated
spectral-response kernel \citep{DarveauBernierEtAl2022}; legacy trace
products are not used.

Two calibration masks with different counts coexist by construction: the
source-curvature calibration uses all 102 event-complement integrations,
while the baseline mask of the spatial-response build retains 101
integrations because its timing and quality manifest excludes one further
sample; the template fit of that build then spans all transit phases in
stratified folds.

\subsection{Detector support and reporting bins}

The initial mapping contained 2{,}726 wavelength groups. Six edge groups
without reliable mapping are excluded (order-1 columns 23, 24, and 2043;
order-2 columns 974, 1677, and 1678), leaving 2{,}720 fitted groups; the
response product records eight further unsupported groups handled by the
runtime support logic.

Order-1 wavelength-calibration training anchors end at detector coordinate
$x=73.62893$, corresponding to $2.75977449\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ for this visit,
so detector columns 38--73 rely on polynomial extrapolation. The
reporting rule of Section~\ref{sec:methods} excludes every bin that
touches these columns: bin 147 is 71.7\% inside the direct domain but is
excluded as a whole, bin 148 lies outside it, and bin 149 was already
structurally excluded from the release's 143 bins, so the rule removes two
retained bins and leaves 141. Bin 146 is the last retained bin and ends at
$2.740018\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.

\subsection{Spectrum parameterisation and orbital constants}

In Equation~\ref{eq:depth-basis} the basis-width weights $w_k$ define the
weighted-zero-mean gauge of the morphology and $\bm{H}_v$ spans the
subspace orthogonal to the constant. The achromatic depth
$D_{\mathrm{global}}$ is bounded below by a floor of $10^{-6}$ ppm plus a
bound margin of $10^{-8}$ ppm in the internal transformed
parameterisation. The order-discrepancy coordinates $\Delta$ are bounded
at $\pm 10^{-12}$: their terminal values sit at this numerical scale and
carry no physical freedom.

The orbital state is: period $3.73548546$~d;
$a/R_\star=17.59002069137651$; impact parameter $0.33859071787462686$;
mid-transit time $60023.52874613816$~$\mathrm{BJD_{TDB}}$; zero
eccentricity and zero argument of periastron. The transit engine uses backend quadrature order 10 beneath
the 21-point finite-exposure average. The limb-darkening priors use
theory scale 0.5 in logit space and curvature scale 2.0 per
$\mu\mathrm{m}^2$ around the STAGGER reference \citep{MagicEtAl2015};
fine nodes outside the valid theory support are excluded rather than
extrapolated.

\subsection{Spatial-response admission gates and known limitation}

For the per-pixel fit of Equation~\ref{eq:qstar-fit}, pixels qualify when
at least 95\% of their out-of-transit samples are valid, their baseline is
positive, and they lie above the 35th percentile of eligible baseline
brightness; the robust fit uses Huber threshold 1.345 with four solve and
reweight cycles. A robust second-difference penalty smooths the response
along detector column and integer trace-relative rank, with strength
chosen by opposite-fold chi-squared, followed by fixed two-fold stability
gates: 95th-percentile half-L1 flux-distribution difference at most 0.15
and centroid difference at most 0.5 pixel.

The documented limitation of the frozen response is quantified by release
audits: 14 centroid resets larger than 0.5 pixel, a median 0.234-pixel
registration difference relative to the delivered source, and roughly
1--2\% excess in-mask red response amplitude at the faintest columns. Any
prospective repair is bound by the closure rules of
Appendix~\ref{app:benchmarking}.

\subsection{Background and uncertainty constants}

The background's eight spatial maps comprise a standardised calibration
background anchor plus seven broad polynomial surfaces
($1, y, x, xy, y^2, x^2, xy^2$), orthonormalised on safe off-trace
pixels; ranks 2, 4, and 8 were assessed by blocked off-trace
cross-validation, and rank 8 is the smallest whose worst-fold prediction
error lies within 5\% of the family minimum. The selected temporal
component is the first principal component with absolute
transit-reference correlation at most 0.25; the chosen component had
correlation $-0.0735$.

The per-order uncertainty scales are $N_1=3.0746905465$ and
$N_2=4.1917779190$. The per-row guard is
\begin{equation}
 s_{r(p)}=\max\!\left[1,\;
 \sqrt{\frac{\sum r_{\mathrm{cv}}^2}{\max(n_{\mathrm{cv}}-4,\,1)}}\right],
\end{equation}
from bidirectional out-of-transit cross-validation of a weighted level
plus slope per row.

\subsection{Source-curvature selection scores}

Held-out standardised chi-squared was 3.26065 for the null model, 3.21178
for the common factor, and 3.15235 for the per-order factor. The gates
required the common factor to improve the null by at least 1\%, and the
per-order factor additionally to improve the common one by at least 2\%
and to improve each order individually; the per-order candidate improved
the common score by only 1.85\%, short of the aggregate requirement, and
its order-2 score (1.0226) was worse than the common counterpart
(0.99964), so the common factor was selected. The frozen factor has unit
out-of-transit mean, range 0.999866573--1.000079673, event-mean
correction $-111.390$ ppm, maximum excursion 133.427 ppm, and fold
correlation 0.999921.

\subsection{Exact profiled derivatives and robust loss}

For the inner solve of Equation~\ref{eq:varpro}, write the weighted
augmented residual in its algebraic form
$\bm{r}(\bm{\theta})=\bm{C}(\bm{\theta})\widehat{\bm{\eta}}-\bm{d}$, where
$\bm{\eta}$ stacks $(\bm{\beta},\bm{\alpha})$ and $\bm{C}$ and $\bm{d}$
include the detector standardisation and the anchor rows. This algebraic
convention is model minus data; the science residual of
Equation~\ref{eq:residual} is data minus model, and the two signs must
not be mixed when reading the derivatives. Differentiating the normal
equations for nonlinear coordinate $\theta_a$, with
$\bm{C}_a=\partial\bm{C}/\partial\theta_a$,
\begin{equation}
 (\bm{C}^{\mathsf T}\bm{C})\,\bm{\eta}_a
 =-\left(\bm{C}_a^{\mathsf T}\bm{r}
 +\bm{C}^{\mathsf T}\bm{C}_a\widehat{\bm{\eta}}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \bm{r}_a=\bm{C}_a\widehat{\bm{\eta}}+\bm{C}\,\bm{\eta}_a .
\end{equation}
Both continuum and background coefficients respond to $\bm{\theta}$
through these equations; treating them as fixed would omit part of the
derivative and can change the nonlinear step. Sufficient statistics are
streamed, so no dense detector Jacobian is formed.

The robust loss on the standardised residual $z$ is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\delta}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
  \tfrac{1}{2}z^2, & |z|\leq\delta,\\
  \delta\left(|z|-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\right), & |z|>\delta,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\delta=1.345$, implemented as exact outer iteratively reweighted
least squares: weights update outside the inner least-squares solve, so
the inner problem stays linear and the estimator is an exact M-estimator.

\subsection{Material-descent audit and evaluation order}

After each trust-region termination, a deterministic material-descent
audit evaluates the executable objective along scale-normalised Cauchy
and Gauss-Newton directions at linearised residual radii 0.03, 0.1, 0.3,
and 1.0; a physically valid improvement beyond the relative material
threshold, whose executable value is the relative tolerance scaled by
$\max(1,|\mathrm{objective}|)$, restarts the same solver. The ordinary
relative tolerance and restart cap are $10^{-8}$ and 6, and the final
confirmation uses $10^{-10}$ and 12. An accepted fit must show no tested
material feasible descent.

Truth values are unavailable to all preprocessing, fitting, starting,
response selection, mask construction, and convergence code. Once a
terminal detector prediction is immutable, an evaluation sidecar computes
the spectral scores of Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Because the fitted
objective is the detector-space robust likelihood, intermediate truth
error need not fall monotonically even while the fitted objective
improves.

\subsection{Rejected or unpromoted model classes}

The following classes were tested and are retained as negative results:
independent or priored per-order depth discrepancy; per-column priors on
continuum coefficients; aggregate order-contrast priors;
residual-location recentering with wavelength slopes; constant-recentering
families superseded by the frozen curvature factor; median source profiles
vulnerable to background contamination; extra background temporal modes; a
global narrow-response swap; local wavelength-specific smoothing patches;
recovery-side explicit order-overlap modelling; local transit-amplitude
templates at the red end; and continuous-coordinate response candidates
that improved red morphology while degrading order 2 or the headline
gates. None of these is part of NOVA-S; each is recorded with the
evidence that rejected it, so failed ideas are neither repeated nor
quietly omitted.
